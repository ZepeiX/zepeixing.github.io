### [How much mass does a star keep when its companion feeds it?](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2026ApJ..1004...94X/abstract)

When one star in a binary transfers mass to its companion, the companion spins up very quickly. Standard models stop accretion once it reaches critical rotation, so most of the transferred mass is lost. We propose a **disk–star coupling** model in which an accretion or decretion disk regulates how much angular momentum the accretor receives, so it can keep growing even while spinning near break-up. The resulting accretion efficiency depends on rotation and mass ratio, and, when a disk forms, also on the stellar radius and orbital separation. Applied to **Be + subdwarf O/B binaries**, the model produces more massive Be stars, in better agreement with observations.

### [Black holes in the "mass gap"](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...989..188X/abstract)

The gravitational-wave event **GW230529** involved a compact object heavier than any known neutron star but lighter than most black holes, in the so-called mass gap. Using POSYDON, we study how neutron star–black hole binaries form through isolated binary evolution and whether they can produce such mass-gap black holes. A very efficient common-envelope phase pushes black hole masses toward the gap, but once we account for selection effects in low-mass X-ray binaries, which point to a maximum neutron star birth mass near 2 M<sub>☉</sub>, that is no longer needed. Depending on the neutron star equation of state, roughly **4–30%** of observable mergers could have an electromagnetic counterpart.

### [Why do black holes in X-ray binaries spin so fast?](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025A%26A...693A..27X/abstract)

The three confirmed wind-fed black hole high-mass X-ray binaries (**Cygnus X-1, LMC X-1 and M33 X-7**) all appear to host rapidly spinning black holes, while theory and gravitational-wave observations suggest first-born black holes spin slowly. With POSYDON population synthesis at solar metallicity, testing Eddington-limited, moderately super-Eddington and fully conservative accretion, we find these systems have most likely **already gone through Roche-lobe overflow after the black hole formed**. Reaching extreme spins requires highly conservative accretion. Our models favour donors of about 10–20 M<sub>☉</sub>, in tension with Cygnus X-1's ~40 M<sub>☉</sub> donor, which hints that mass transfer before the black hole forms may be more conservative than usually assumed.

### [From the main sequence to merger: neutron star–black hole binaries](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024A%26A...683A.144X/abstract)

We follow merging neutron star–black hole binaries at solar metallicity from the zero-age main sequence to merger with **POSYDON**, which is built on detailed single- and binary-star models rather than approximate recipes. In our default model, most merging systems have black holes of 3–11 M<sub>☉</sub> and chirp masses of 1.5–4 M<sub>☉</sub>. The black hole always forms first, with a spin of at most about 0.2 that correlates with the initial orbital period, and it can be spun up moderately by later mass transfer. About **40%** of systems formed only through stable mass transfer have anti-aligned black hole spins, and 0–18.6% of mergers may produce an electromagnetic counterpart.
